The Ziz Valley: Morocco's Palm Oasis Road

Between the last mountain passes and the first dunes, the road drops into the Ziz Valley — a narrow gorge that opens into one of Morocco's longest palm oases, and one of the best stretches of the drive to the Sahara.

Planning · 6 min read · Morocco Boutique Tours

A River That Shaped the Route South

The Ziz River rises in the High Atlas and flows southeast toward the Sahara, and for centuries the valley it carved has shaped the route between Fes and the desert oases beyond. The road today largely follows the same path traders and caravans once used, dropping out of the mountains through the Ziz Gorges — a section of tight switchbacks and tunnels blasted through rock in the 20th century — before the valley opens out into wide palm groves.

The Ziz Gorges

North of Errachidia, the road threads through the Ziz Gorges, where the valley narrows and the river cuts close beside the roadway between steep rock walls. Several viewpoints along this stretch offer a chance to stop and look back down the gorge — one of the more dramatic short sections of driving on the route between Fes and Merzouga, and a natural break in a long day on the road.

The Palm Oasis

South of the gorges, the valley widens and the Ziz oasis begins — a nearly continuous run of date palms stretching roughly 15 kilometers (about 9 miles) alongside the river, dotted with small kasbahs and villages built from the same reddish earth as the surrounding hills. It's one of the longest continuous palm oases in Morocco, and a striking sight from the road above, particularly in the late afternoon light. Dates from this region, especially around the town of Erfoud, are considered among Morocco's best.

Where the Ziz Valley Fits on the Map

The Ziz Valley sits directly on the standard route between Fes (or Midelt) and Merzouga, meaning it requires no detour to see — it's simply part of the drive south to the Sahara. Most itineraries that include a night in the Merzouga dunes pass through the valley in one direction or both, making it one of the more memorable, unplanned highlights of a Sahara-bound trip rather than a separate stop to research.

A Good Stretching Point

Given how long the drive from Fes to Merzouga can be, the Ziz Valley's viewpoints double as a natural place to stop, stretch, and take photographs partway through the journey — usually alongside a roadside café or two overlooking the gorge, where mint tea and a break from the car are easy to find.

Frequently Asked

The Ziz Valley: Quick Answers

What is the Ziz Valley?

The Ziz Valley is the route the Ziz River carves through southeastern Morocco on its way toward the Sahara, running through a narrow gorge in the Atlas foothills before opening into one of the country's longest continuous palm oases.

Where is the Ziz Valley located?

It sits along the main road between Fes and Merzouga, roughly between the towns of Errachidia and Erfoud, making it a highlight of the drive rather than a separate destination to plan around.

How long is the Ziz Valley palm oasis?

The oasis running alongside the Ziz River stretches for roughly 15 kilometers (about 9 miles), making it one of the longest continuous palm groves in Morocco.

Is the Ziz Valley worth stopping for?

Yes — a viewpoint overlooking the gorge and oasis is a natural photo stop on the road to Merzouga, and requires no detour since it sits directly on the standard Fes-to-Sahara route.
