Fes Medina & Tanneries: A First-Timer's Guide to the Old City

Fes el-Bali is the largest car-free urban area in the world, with more than 9,000 lanes threading through a medina that has barely changed shape in a thousand years. It's extraordinary — and, without a guide, genuinely easy to get lost in.

Destinations · 7 min read · Morocco Boutique Tours

Navigating the World's Largest Car-Free Old City

Fes el-Bali, the old medina, has no cars, no straight streets, and very few landmarks visible above the low rooftops, which is part of what has preserved its medieval character so completely. First-time visitors, ourselves included when we first came here, reliably get turned around within minutes — which is exactly why a local guide, standard on our Fes itineraries, transforms the experience from disorienting to genuinely illuminating.

The Chouara Tannery

Fes's leather tanneries, and the Chouara Tannery in particular, are among the oldest in the world, using techniques largely unchanged for centuries. Visitors view the honeycomb of stone dye and treatment vats from surrounding terraces, where hides are cured, then dyed in vivid natural colors before becoming the bags, poufs, and leather goods sold throughout the medina. Shop staff typically offer a sprig of fresh mint on arrival — a genuinely practical gesture, given the tannery's strong, distinctive smell.

Al-Qarawiyyin: The World's Oldest University

Founded in 859 AD, Al-Qarawiyyin is recognized by UNESCO and Guinness World Records as the oldest continuously operating institution of higher learning in the world. Its library and mosque are not open to non-Muslim visitors internally, but the ornate exterior gates and courtyard glimpses from the street are worth seeking out.

Bou Inania Madrasa

One of the few religious buildings in Fes open to non-Muslim visitors, this 14th-century Quranic school is a showcase of Marinid-era craftsmanship — intricate carved cedar, zellige tilework, and calligraphy across its courtyard walls, offering a rare interior look at the decorative artistry found throughout the medina's private buildings.

Souk Etiquette & Bargaining

Fes's souks are organized loosely by trade — a spice quarter, a textile quarter, a metalwork quarter — a layout dating back centuries. Bargaining is expected for most handicrafts and is generally good-natured rather than aggressive; a polite counter-offer around half the initial asking price is a reasonable starting point for negotiation, adjusting from there.

Why a Local Guide Is Worth It in Fes

More than almost anywhere else in Morocco, Fes rewards local expertise — a good guide won't just prevent you from getting lost, but will read centuries of history into doorways, courtyards, and workshops you'd otherwise walk straight past. All our Fes itineraries include a guided medina walking tour for exactly this reason.

Frequently Asked

Visiting Fes: Quick Answers

Is Fes medina safe to visit?

Yes, Fes el-Bali is generally safe for visitors, though its maze-like layout means getting lost is common without a guide. As in any busy medina, keep an eye on belongings in crowded lanes.

Do you need a guide in Fes medina?

It isn't strictly required, but is strongly recommended — Fes el-Bali has over 9,000 lanes with few visible landmarks, and a local guide adds substantial historical and cultural context you would otherwise miss entirely.

What is the smell at the Chouara Tannery?

The tanning process uses natural but strong-smelling materials, including lime and pigeon droppings, as part of the traditional curing process. Tannery shops typically offer visitors a sprig of fresh mint to hold while viewing the vats from the terraces.

How many days do you need in Fes?

One full day with a guided medina tour covers the essential highlights; two days allows a more relaxed pace and time to explore the Ville Nouvelle or take a day trip to Chefchaouen.
