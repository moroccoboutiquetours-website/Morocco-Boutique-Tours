Hassan II Mosque: Visiting Casablanca's Landmark

Built out over the Atlantic on reclaimed land, the Hassan II Mosque is Casablanca's defining landmark — and one of the very few mosques in Morocco that non-Muslim visitors can step inside.

Destinations · 6 min read · Morocco Boutique Tours

Why the Hassan II Mosque Stands Out

Completed in 1993, the Hassan II Mosque was built partly on land reclaimed from the Atlantic, so that — in keeping with a verse from the Quran — the faithful could pray over the water. Its minaret rises roughly 210 meters (689 feet), making it the tallest minaret in the world, visible from much of the city and from ships approaching the port. The mosque was designed by French architect Michel Pinseau and built largely with Moroccan materials and craftsmanship: cedar from the Middle Atlas, marble, granite, and hand-carved plasterwork and zellige tilework produced by thousands of local artisans.

One of the Only Mosques Open to Non-Muslims

Most working mosques in Morocco are closed to non-Muslim visitors, which makes the Hassan II Mosque something of an exception — and one of the main reasons it draws visitors who might otherwise fly straight through Casablanca on their way elsewhere. Guided tours, led in several languages, take visitors through the vast prayer hall (capable of holding tens of thousands of worshippers), up to view the retractable roof, and down to the hammam and ablution rooms beneath the main floor, where geometric fountains and further tilework are on display.

Visiting Hours and What to Expect

Tours typically run several times a day outside of the five daily prayer times, when the mosque closes briefly to visitors. During Ramadan and on Fridays, visiting hours are more limited. Modest dress is required — shoulders and knees covered for all visitors — and shoes come off before entering the prayer hall itself. The scale of the interior is the main draw: soaring painted ceilings, an enormous chandelier-lit prayer hall, and a level of detail in the tilework and carved plaster that takes considerably longer to absorb than the visit itself allows.

Where It Fits in a Casablanca Visit

Casablanca is Morocco's largest city and, for many visitors, the first stop after landing at Mohammed V International Airport — which makes the Hassan II Mosque a natural first landmark on a private tour, whether as a half-day stop before heading to Rabat, Fes, or Marrakech, or as the highlight of a shorter Casablanca stay before flying out. The mosque's oceanfront corniche, lined with cafés, is also a pleasant place to walk afterward and see the building from a distance, particularly around sunset.

Frequently Asked

Visiting the Hassan II Mosque: Quick Answers

Can non-Muslims visit the Hassan II Mosque?

Yes — the Hassan II Mosque is one of the only mosques in Morocco open to non-Muslim visitors, accessible on a guided tour that includes the main prayer hall and the ablution room below it.

How big is the Hassan II Mosque?

It is one of the largest mosques in the world by capacity, with a minaret roughly 210 meters (689 feet) tall — the tallest minaret in the world — and a prayer hall that can hold tens of thousands of worshippers.

Do I need to book a tour in advance?

Guided tours run several times a day outside of prayer times, and while walk-up visits are often possible, booking or arriving early is recommended during busier travel seasons.

What should I wear to visit the mosque?

Modest dress is required for all visitors — shoulders and knees covered — and shoes are removed before entering the prayer hall, in keeping with mosque etiquette.
