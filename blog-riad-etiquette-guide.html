A Guide to Riad Etiquette: What to Know Before You Stay

A riad isn't just a place to sleep — it's a traditional Moroccan house built inward around a courtyard, and staying in one is part of the experience. Here's what to expect.

Culture · 6 min read · Morocco Boutique Tours

What Exactly Is a Riad?

A riad is a traditional Moroccan townhouse built around an interior courtyard or garden, historically designed to keep family life private from the street outside — hence the plain, windowless exterior walls typical of medina architecture. Inside, most riads center on a courtyard with a small pool or fountain, carved plasterwork, and a rooftop terrace, usually converted into a small guesthouse of just a handful of rooms.

Settling In: Arrival Customs

Most riads greet arriving guests with a glass of mint tea in the courtyard while your host handles check-in — there's rarely a front desk in the hotel sense. Shoes are often removed at the door or before stepping onto carpeted lounge areas; if you're unsure, watch what your host does, or simply ask.

Courtyard Living & Privacy

Because riads are built around a shared central courtyard, common areas can feel more intimate than a hotel lobby — you'll likely cross paths with other guests at breakfast or in the lounge. Voices carry more than in a conventional hotel, so a slightly quieter volume in shared spaces is appreciated, particularly in the evening.

Rooftop Terraces

The rooftop terrace is often a riad's best feature — a private, open-air space above the medina's rooftops, ideal for breakfast, sunset, or simply escaping the narrow lanes below for a while. Terraces are usually shared among guests, so it's worth checking with your host about quieter times if you'd like it to yourself.

Breakfast & Daily Rhythm

Breakfast is typically included and served in the courtyard or on the terrace — expect fresh bread, msemen (Moroccan flatbread), honey, olive oil, jam, and Moroccan coffee or tea. Riads are generally quieter and more personal than large hotels, so it's normal to chat briefly with your host about the day ahead; many are happy to point you toward a good spot for lunch or a particular corner of the souks.

Tipping & Dress Code

A small tip for housekeeping or the person managing your stay is appreciated but not obligatory — 20-50 MAD per day is a reasonable guide. Dress inside the riad is relaxed, but once you step out into the medina, modest clothing (shoulders and knees covered) is respectful, particularly near mosques and in more traditional neighborhoods.

Frequently Asked

Riad Stays: Quick Answers

What is the difference between a riad and a hotel?

A riad is a small, traditional guesthouse converted from a historic courtyard home, usually with just a handful of rooms, personal service, and no street-facing windows. A hotel is typically larger, more standardized, and built specifically as commercial lodging.

Do you tip riad staff?

Tipping isn't mandatory but is customary and appreciated. 20-50 MAD per day for housekeeping, or a similar amount for a host who has been especially helpful, is a good guide.

Is there wifi in Moroccan riads?

Most riads catering to international travelers, including all those we work with, offer wifi throughout, though signal in thick courtyard walls can occasionally be weaker in individual rooms than in shared lounge areas.

Can non-Muslims stay in riads?

Yes — riads are simply traditional guesthouses open to all travelers regardless of religion, and staying in one is one of the most popular ways to experience Morocco.
