What to Pack for the Atlas Mountains & Sahara Desert

On a single day crossing the High Atlas to the Sahara, you might feel cool mountain air, blazing desert sun, and a cold desert night — all before dinner. Layering is everything.

Packing · 6 min read · Morocco Boutique Tours

The Layering Principle

The single most useful packing habit for a Morocco road trip is dressing in light layers you can add or remove throughout the day. A private, chauffeur-driven itinerary through the High Atlas and the Sahara can move through several distinct micro-climates in a matter of hours — a cool mountain pass in the morning, intense desert sun by midday, and a genuinely cold night at camp.

Desert Heat vs. Desert Cold

Daytime in the Sahara can be very hot, especially from May through September, so breathable, loose-fitting clothing and a wide-brimmed hat or scarf for sun protection go a long way. Once the sun sets, temperatures drop fast — even in summer, desert camp evenings call for a fleece or light jacket, and in winter (December to February) you'll want a proper warm layer, since nighttime temperatures can approach freezing.

Crossing the High Atlas

The Tizi n'Tichka pass, crossed on most Marrakech-Sahara routes, sits at roughly 2,260 meters, and mountain weather can shift quickly regardless of season. A light waterproof or windproof layer is worth having in your day bag even if it's warm when you set off.

Footwear

Comfortable, closed walking shoes suit most of the itinerary — kasbah lanes, gorge walks, and medina cobblestones all reward good grip. For the camel trek and desert camp itself, sandals or slip-on shoes are useful, since sand gets into everything; some travelers simply go barefoot for the final stretch into camp.

Modesty in Medinas & Mosques

Morocco is relaxed about clothing overall, but modest dress (shoulders and knees covered) is appreciated in medinas, souks, and especially near mosques, most of which are not open to non-Muslim visitors for interior viewing. A light scarf is genuinely useful — for sun, wind-blown sand, and modesty around religious sites all at once.

Quick Packing List

  • Breathable, loose clothing for daytime desert and city heat
  • A warm layer (fleece or light down jacket) for desert camp evenings and Atlas mountain crossings
  • A light scarf or shemagh for sun, wind, and modesty
  • Comfortable closed walking shoes, plus sandals for camp
  • Sunglasses and a high-SPF sunscreen
  • A reusable water bottle
  • A basic first-aid kit with rehydration salts
  • A portable phone charger or power bank for long driving days
  • Cash in Moroccan dirhams for small purchases and tips
Frequently Asked

Packing for Morocco: Quick Answers

Is it cold in the Sahara Desert at night?

Yes, even in summer nights cool considerably after sunset since sand loses heat quickly. In winter, temperatures at desert camps can drop close to freezing, so a warm layer is essential year-round.

What shoes should I wear for Ait Benhaddou and Merzouga?

Comfortable closed walking shoes work well for kasbah lanes and gorge walks. For the dunes themselves and the camel trek into camp, sandals or slip-on shoes are more practical, since sand gets everywhere in closed shoes.

Do I need to bring a scarf in Morocco?

A scarf is genuinely useful rather than just a souvenir — for sun protection, blowing sand near the dunes, and covering up respectfully near mosques and in more traditional neighborhoods.
